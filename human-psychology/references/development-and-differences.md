# Development and Individual Differences

## Child development
- **Attachment** forms in infancy; sensitive, responsive caregiving predicts secure attachment (moderate-strong; effects on later outcomes are real but modest).
- **Piaget's stages** (sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational): broad sequence is useful, but children show abilities earlier than Piaget thought and development is more gradual and domain-specific.
- **Vygotsky**: learning happens best in the zone of proximal development with support (scaffolding).
- **Theory of mind** emerges around age 4 (false-belief tasks), earlier with implicit measures.
- **Self-control** grows with age; the marshmallow test's predictive power shrinks a lot after controlling for family background (Watts et al. 2018).
- **Play** supports learning, social skills, and self-regulation.

## Adolescence
- Reward systems are highly sensitive while prefrontal control is still maturing (dual systems model, debated in details); peers strongly increase risk-taking in this period (Steinberg).
- Strong drive for status, belonging, autonomy, and identity exploration.
- Sleep timing shifts later.
- Design and messaging for teens: respect autonomy, avoid lecturing, avoid exploiting social comparison or variable rewards with money.

## Adulthood and aging
- Personality traits become more stable; conscientiousness and agreeableness tend to rise, neuroticism to fall (maturity principle).
- **Fluid intelligence** (speed, novel problems) peaks in early adulthood and declines; **crystallized intelligence** (knowledge, vocabulary) holds or rises into later life.
- **Socioemotional selectivity theory** (Carstensen): older adults prioritize emotionally meaningful goals and show a positivity effect in attention and memory.
- Older adults are targeted by scams partly due to social isolation, trust norms, and sometimes cognitive decline.

## Intelligence
- General intelligence (g) underlies performance across diverse tests and strongly predicts education and job performance. Heritability rises with age (to roughly 50-80% in adulthood in high-income samples), but environment matters (Flynn effect, schooling raises IQ).
- Multiple intelligences (Gardner) lacks empirical support as separate intelligences; emotional intelligence as ability has some validity, as a trait overlaps heavily with personality.

## Personality
- **Big Five** traits are moderately heritable, fairly stable, and predict real outcomes (health, job performance, relationships). Effects are modest but meaningful.
- **Dark Triad** (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy): socially aversive traits linked to manipulation and exploitation; useful for recognizing patterns, not for diagnosing people.
- MBTI types: popular, but poor reliability and not supported as distinct types; Big Five is the scientific alternative.

## Neurodiversity (general information)
- **ADHD**: differences in attention regulation, impulsivity, and reward timing; immediate rewards and novelty are especially motivating, delayed rewards less so. Helpful design: clear short steps, external reminders, quick feedback, reducing distraction.
- **Autism**: differences in social communication and sensory processing, preference for predictability. Helpful design: clear explicit instructions, predictable structure, sensory control options.
- **Dyslexia**: reading difficulty with typically normal intelligence; accessible fonts and audio options help.
These are general patterns; individuals vary widely. Do not diagnose.

## Sex and gender differences
Average differences exist on some traits (e.g., interest in people vs things, agreeableness, neuroticism, physical aggression) but distributions overlap heavily; most cognitive differences are small. Avoid stereotyping individuals from averages.
