# Clinical Psychology Basics and Wellbeing (general information, not diagnosis)

Use for explaining how common conditions work psychologically and what helps in general. For a real person's symptoms, suggest a licensed professional. Crisis situations need crisis services, not this file.

## Anxiety
- Core loop: perceived threat -> fear response -> avoidance -> short-term relief -> avoidance is reinforced (negative reinforcement) -> fear never gets disconfirmed.
- Cognitive patterns: overestimating danger, underestimating coping, intolerance of uncertainty, worry as an attempt at control.
- Effective treatments: CBT with **exposure** (gradually facing feared situations so the brain learns safety; strong evidence), medication for some people.

## Depression
- Features: low mood or loss of interest, fatigue, sleep and appetite change, hopelessness, self-criticism.
- Loops: withdrawal reduces rewarding experiences, which deepens low mood; rumination maintains it.
- Effective approaches: **behavioral activation** (scheduling meaningful, rewarding activities; strong), CBT, interpersonal therapy, medication, exercise as an adjunct (moderate-strong). The "chemical imbalance" explanation is an oversimplification.

## Addiction and compulsive behavior
- Substances and some behaviors hijack learning: repeated dopamine surges strengthen cue-triggered **wanting** (incentive sensitization; Berridge & Robinson), while **liking** may fade (tolerance). Habits shift control toward automatic, cue-driven responses; stress and withdrawal add negative reinforcement (using to escape bad feelings).
- Gambling disorder is the best-established behavioral addiction; gaming disorder is recognized by WHO; "social media addiction" is debated as a category, though problematic use is real for some people.
- What helps: changing cues and environment, replacing routines, social support, Motivational Interviewing, CBT, contingency management (rewards for abstinence; strong evidence for stimulants), medications for some substance use disorders.

## Stress and coping
- Lazarus & Folkman: stress depends on appraisal of threat vs coping resources.
- Problem-focused coping (fixing the problem) works for controllable stressors; emotion-focused coping (reappraisal, support) for uncontrollable ones.
- Social support is one of the strongest buffers.

## Wellbeing (what reliably helps on average)
Sleep, physical activity, social connection, meaningful goals, time in nature (moderate), gratitude and kindness practices (small effects), reducing chronic stress. Money raises wellbeing, with diminishing returns but no hard cap in recent data (Killingsworth 2021; Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers 2023 adversarial collaboration).
- **Positive psychology interventions** have small effects after correcting for publication bias.
- **Mindfulness** helps modestly for stress and anxiety; not a cure-all.

## Trauma and resilience
Most people exposed to trauma do not develop PTSD; resilience is common (Bonanno). Evidence-based PTSD treatments include trauma-focused CBT, prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy, and EMDR. Immediate mandatory "debriefing" after trauma can be unhelpful.

## Cautions
- Don't diagnose from conversations.
- Avoid pop terms used loosely ("toxic," "narcissist," "trauma" for ordinary stress).
- Self-help claims often outrun evidence; give evidence grades.
