# Social Psychology

## How we explain behavior (attribution)
- **Fundamental attribution error / correspondence bias**: overweighting personality and underweighting situation when explaining others' actions (robust in Western samples; weaker in some cultures).
- **Actor-observer asymmetry**: smaller and less consistent than textbooks claim (Malle 2006 meta-analysis).
- **Self-serving bias**: credit success to self, failure to circumstances (robust).
- **Hostile attribution bias**: reading ambiguous acts as hostile; linked to aggression.

## Conformity, norms, and obedience
- **Asch conformity**: people often go along with a clearly wrong majority; rates have declined since the 1950s and vary by culture; one dissenter sharply reduces conformity.
- **Informational vs normative influence**: conforming to be right vs to be liked.
- **Descriptive norms** (what people do) vs **injunctive norms** (what people approve). Messages saying "many people do the bad thing" can backfire by normalizing it.
- **Pluralistic ignorance**: everyone privately disagrees but assumes others agree (campus drinking studies).
- **Milgram obedience**: high compliance under authority; later analyses show participants obeyed less when orders were explicit commands and more when they identified with the scientific goal ("engaged followership"). Real but interpreted differently now.
- **Stanford Prison Experiment**: not valid evidence; guards were coached.

## Groups
- **Social identity theory** (Tajfel & Turner): people derive self-worth from groups; minimal groups (random assignment) still produce in-group favoritism (robust).
- **Groupthink**: cohesion plus pressure suppresses dissent; the formal model has mixed support, but devil's advocates and anonymous input do improve decisions.
- **Group polarization**: discussion among like-minded people pushes views more extreme (robust).
- **Social loafing**: less individual effort in groups when contributions aren't identifiable (robust); fix by making contributions visible and meaningful.
- **Social facilitation**: an audience improves easy, well-learned tasks and impairs hard, new ones.
- **Deindividuation**: anonymity increases conformity to group norms, which can be good or bad.

## Prejudice and intergroup relations
- Stereotypes, prejudice (feelings), discrimination (behavior).
- **Contact hypothesis** (Allport): positive contact under equal status, common goals, cooperation, and institutional support reduces prejudice (robust; Pettigrew & Tropp meta-analysis).
- **Implicit Association Test**: measures associations but predicts individual behavior weakly and has low test-retest reliability; don't treat scores as diagnoses.
- **Stereotype threat**: effects shrank substantially in large preregistered replications; treat as small and context-dependent.

## Helping and cooperation
- **Bystander effect**: more bystanders reduce individual helping in some settings, but real-world CCTV analyses show someone intervenes in about 90% of public conflicts (Philpot et al. 2020).
- **Empathy-altruism** and reciprocity both drive helping; people help more when the need is clear, they feel responsible, and helping is easy.
- **Identifiable victim effect**: one named person moves people more than statistics (moderate).
- Cooperation is sustained by reputation, reciprocity, and punishment of free riders (public goods games).

## Attraction and relationships
- Proximity, familiarity, similarity, physical attractiveness, and reciprocal liking predict initial attraction.
- **Gottman's research**: contempt, criticism, defensiveness, and stonewalling predict relationship breakdown; positive-to-negative interaction ratio matters. Practitioner-adjacent; prediction claims have been criticized for overfitting.
- **Investment model** (Rusbult): commitment = satisfaction + investments - quality of alternatives.
- Responsiveness (feeling understood, valued, cared for) is a core ingredient of close relationships.

## Cognitive dissonance
Holding conflicting beliefs or acting against beliefs creates discomfort that people reduce by changing attitudes, adding justifications, or minimizing. Effort justification (valuing things we suffered for) and post-decision spreading (liking chosen options more) are robust in core form.

## Culture
Most psychology research comes from WEIRD samples (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic; Henrich et al. 2010). Individualism-collectivism, tightness-looseness, and honor cultures shift conformity, attribution, self-enhancement, and emotion norms. Always ask whether a finding generalizes to the audience at hand.
