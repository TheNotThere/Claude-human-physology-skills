# The Human Mind in Detail: How Thinking Works

A component-by-component map of cognition, from sensing to self. Each part notes key brain regions and evidence status. Read with mind-models.md (theories) and brain-and-reward.md (reward).

## 1. Perception
- Raw signals are ambiguous; the brain combines them with expectations (predictive processing, Bayesian perception). Illusions show the assumptions it uses.
- Gestalt grouping (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, figure-ground).
- Separate pathways: visual "what" (ventral, temporal lobe) and "where/how" (dorsal, parietal).
- Multisensory integration: the brain weights each sense by reliability (ventriloquist effect, McGurk effect).
- Perception is selective: change blindness and inattentional blindness show we see far less than we think.

## 2. Attention
- Bottom-up capture by salience (novelty, motion, contrast, faces, own name, threat) vs top-down goal-driven focus.
- Networks: dorsal attention (goal orienting), ventral attention (interrupts for unexpected events), salience network (switches between internal and external focus), alerting (noradrenaline), executive control.
- Limits: attentional blink, dual-task costs, roughly 4-item working memory.
- Sustained attention declines over time (vigilance decrement), especially on boring tasks.

## 3. Working memory and executive function
- Executive functions (Miyake): updating working memory, inhibition, shifting between tasks. Mainly prefrontal and parietal networks.
- Supports planning, reasoning, delaying gratification, and overriding habits.
- Develops through adolescence into the mid-20s; impaired by stress, sleep loss, alcohol, and overload.

## 4. Long-term memory
Encoding, consolidation, storage, retrieval, reconsolidation. Episodic (events; hippocampus), semantic (facts; temporal cortex), procedural (skills; basal ganglia, cerebellum), priming and conditioning. Memory is reconstructive and suggestible. Detail in memory-and-learning.md.

## 5. Concepts and knowledge
Categories form around prototypes and examples; schemas and scripts (e.g., a "restaurant script") guide expectations and fill gaps, which also produces memory errors.

## 6. Language
Speech perception, word retrieval, syntax, meaning, pragmatics (intended meaning). Left-lateralized networks including inferior frontal and superior temporal areas, but language is distributed. Framing and word choice shape attention and judgment, but strong "language determines thought" claims are not supported; weak effects (e.g., color categories) are.

## 7. Reasoning and problem solving
- Deductive reasoning is error-prone in abstract form and better with familiar content.
- Inductive reasoning and analogy drive most real-world learning.
- Problem solving: representing the problem, searching options, using heuristics (means-end analysis), insight (sudden restructuring, often after an impasse; incubation helps modestly).
- Motivated reasoning: people evaluate evidence more harshly when it threatens their beliefs or identity (strong).

## 8. Judgment and decision-making
Heuristics (availability, representativeness, anchoring, affect) are efficient but biased. Value is computed in ventromedial prefrontal and orbitofrontal cortex with striatum; uncertainty and conflict involve anterior cingulate and insula. Detail in decisions.md.

## 9. Emotion
Rapid evaluation of significance that prepares body and mind for action; shapes attention, memory, and choices. Amygdala (relevance, threat), insula (body state), orbitofrontal and ventromedial prefrontal cortex (value), anterior cingulate (conflict, pain). Detail in emotion.md.

## 10. Motivation
Wanting (dopamine), liking (opioid hotspots), needs (autonomy, competence, relatedness), goals, and expected value minus effort. Detail in motivation.md.

## 11. Self and identity
- Self-concept, self-esteem, and narrative identity (the life story people tell). Medial prefrontal cortex and default mode network support self-referential thought.
- People maintain a positive and consistent self-image: self-serving bias, cognitive dissonance reduction (Festinger; robust in core form).
- Identity strongly guides behavior ("people like me do this").

## 12. Social mind
Theory of mind, empathy, social learning (imitation), conformity (Asch; effect varies by culture and era), obedience (Milgram; interpretations debated), in-group bias, reputation management, norms. Detail in social-negotiation-language.md.

## 13. Metacognition and consciousness
- Metacognition: monitoring one's own thinking (confidence, feeling of knowing). Often miscalibrated.
- Consciousness theories: global workspace (information becomes conscious when broadcast widely), higher-order theories, integrated information theory, recurrent processing. A 2023 adversarial collaboration tested global workspace against integrated information theory and neither fully passed; the field is unresolved.
- Much processing happens outside awareness, but strong claims about subliminal messages controlling behavior failed replication. Small effects in narrow lab conditions only.

## 14. Development and individual differences
Cognition changes across life (rapid early learning, adolescent reward sensitivity with maturing control, peak processing speed in early adulthood, accumulated knowledge rising into later life). Personality traits (Big Five), intelligence (g; strongly predicts learning and job performance), and culture shape every component above.

## How it fits together (one pass)
Senses + expectations -> perception -> attention selects -> working memory holds -> long-term memory and schemas interpret -> emotion and motivation assign value -> heuristics or deliberate reasoning decide -> action -> outcome feeds back into learning -> the self tells a story about why.
