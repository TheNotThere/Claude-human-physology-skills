# Neurophysiology and Body States

Read for questions about how neurons, chemicals, stress, sleep, hunger, or body signals affect thinking and behavior. Mechanism first, then the behavioral consequence, then how strong the link is.

## Neural signaling (textbook-level; strong)
- **Resting potential** about -70 mV, set mainly by K+ leak channels and the Na+/K+ pump (3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in). The Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equation describes it; Hodgkin & Huxley (1952) remains the reference model for the action potential.
- **Action potential**: threshold around -55 mV. Voltage-gated Na+ channels open (upstroke), then inactivate while voltage-gated K+ channels open (repolarization), producing refractory periods. Lasts about 1-2 ms. Myelin allows saltatory conduction, up to roughly 120 m/s. All-or-none per neuron; intensity is coded by firing rate and how many neurons fire.
- **Synapse**: depolarization opens voltage-gated Ca2+ channels; Ca2+ binds synaptotagmin, triggering SNARE-mediated vesicle fusion. Release is quantal and probabilistic.
- **Receptors**: AMPA (fast excitation); NMDA (needs both glutamate and depolarization to remove its Mg2+ block, so it detects coincidence; key for learning); GABA-A and glycine receptors (inhibition).
- **Plasticity**: long-term potentiation (Bliss & Lomo 1973) depends on NMDA-driven Ca2+ entry, CaMKII, insertion of more AMPA receptors, and for lasting change, new protein synthesis (CREB). Long-term depression weakens synapses. "Neurons that fire together wire together" is the slogan; repetition and timing matter.
- **Reconsolidation**: in animal studies, recalled memories become briefly changeable before restabilizing. In humans, evidence is mixed and effects are boundary-limited. Do not claim it lets anyone reliably "rewrite" memories.

## Neuromodulators (the brain's volume knobs)
- **Dopamine** (VTA, substantia nigra): reward prediction error and wanting. See brain-and-reward.md.
- **Noradrenaline** (locus coeruleus): alertness and arousal; phasic bursts to novel or important events boost attention and memory for them.
- **Serotonin** (raphe nuclei): mood, patience, behavioral inhibition. "Serotonin = happiness" and "depression = low serotonin" are oversimplified.
- **Acetylcholine** (basal forebrain): attention and encoding new information.
- **Orexin/hypocretin and histamine**: keep you awake. **Adenosine** builds up while awake as sleep pressure.
- **Caffeine** blocks adenosine receptors (A1/A2A). It masks sleepiness rather than adding energy; when it wears off, accumulated sleep pressure returns (strong).
- **Oxytocin** is not a general "trust hormone"; early nasal-spray trust findings largely failed to replicate.

## Arousal and performance
- **Yerkes-Dodson** (inverted U): moderate arousal helps performance; too little or too much hurts. Optimal arousal is higher for simple tasks than complex ones. Treat as a useful rule of thumb, not a precise law.

## Stress (HPA axis)
- Hypothalamus releases CRH, the pituitary releases ACTH, and the adrenal glands release cortisol. Cortisol rises 30-45 minutes after waking (cortisol awakening response). Feedback runs through the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex.
- Acute adrenaline/noradrenaline surges prepare the body for action and sharpen memory for emotional events.
- **Chronic stress** impairs prefrontal control and flexible thinking and increases amygdala reactivity and reliance on habits (moderate-strong, much from animal work). Stressed people make faster, more habitual, more short-sighted choices.
- Design implication: a stressed user needs fewer choices, clearer defaults, and reassurance, not more pressure.

## Autonomic nervous system
- Sympathetic (fight/flight) and parasympathetic (rest/digest, mostly via the vagus nerve).
- **Heart rate variability (HRV)** is a reasonable rough index of vagal influence and regulation capacity (moderate).
- **Polyvagal theory's** evolutionary three-stage story is contested; don't present it as established.
- Slow breathing (about 6 breaths/min, longer exhale) reliably lowers arousal for many people (moderate).

## Sleep (strong evidence)
- Cycles of about 90 minutes through light NREM, deep slow-wave sleep, and REM.
- Slow-wave sleep supports consolidation of facts and events; REM is linked to emotional and procedural processing.
- Sleep deprivation reduces prefrontal regulation while amygdala reactivity rises (Yoo et al. 2007): more emotional reactivity, worse self-control, worse attention, and more impulsive choices.
- Teens' body clocks shift later; early school starts conflict with biology.

## Circadian rhythm
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus is the master clock, set by light through melanopsin cells in the retina. Melatonin rises in darkness.
- Most people dip in alertness in early afternoon and very early morning. Schedule demanding thinking for each person's peak.

## Hunger and metabolism
- Hunger increases the value of food cues and can increase impatience in some studies (moderate, mixed). The "hungry judges" parole study has strong alternative explanations, so don't cite it as proof.
- Low blood sugar "draining willpower" did not replicate.

## Temperature
- The claim that holding warm drinks makes people judge others as warmer (Williams & Bargh 2008) failed replication. Comfort matters; magic effects don't.

## Interoception (sensing your own body)
- Body signals travel via vagal and spinal pathways to the brainstem, thalamus, and insula. The anterior insula and anterior cingulate form a "salience" hub (Craig; Critchley).
- Distinguish **interoceptive accuracy** (measured performance, e.g., heartbeat detection; the common heartbeat-counting task has validity problems) from **interoceptive sensibility** (self-reported belief). They often don't match.
- Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis: body-based emotional signals guide decisions. Influential; details debated.
- People often misread the cause of their arousal (misattribution; moderate), which is why context shapes how a feeling is labeled.
