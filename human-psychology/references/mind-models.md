# Models of the Mind

Different lenses describe the same mind. Use whichever helps, but label each by scientific status. None are literal brain maps.

## Scientific models (good support)
- **Predictive processing** (Friston; Clark): the brain constantly predicts incoming signals and updates when predictions fail. Perception is shaped by expectation; surprise draws attention and drives learning. Explains why expectations, framing, and prior beliefs shape what people notice and accept. Powerful framework; the "free-energy principle" version is broad and hard to test.
- **Dual-process thinking** (Kahneman): fast intuitive vs slow deliberate processing. A useful simplification, not two separate systems (Melnikoff & Bargh 2018). Deliberate thinking usually endorses intuitive answers rather than overriding them.
- **Brain networks**: the default mode network (self-reflection, mind-wandering, imagining the future), the frontoparietal control network (focused, deliberate tasks), the salience network (detects what matters and switches between the two). Mind-wandering is common during undemanding tasks.
- **The interpreter and confabulation** (Gazzaniga split-brain work; Nisbett & Wilson 1977): people often lack direct access to the causes of their behavior and generate plausible explanations after the fact. Asking about specific behaviors and situations yields better information than asking "why?" This is a reason for humility, not a license to plant reasons in people.
- **Big Five / HEXACO traits**: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism (plus honesty-humility in HEXACO). The best-validated personality model. Conscientiousness predicts follow-through and job performance. Personality-matched messaging shows small effects that are debated.
- **Attachment theory** (Bowlby, Ainsworth; adult work by Hazan & Shaver): secure, anxious, and avoidant patterns in close relationships. Well-supported for close relationships; not a reliable tool for profiling strangers or customers.

## Clinical and practical frameworks (useful, less tested as theories)
- **Freud's id, ego, superego**: historically important; the metaphor of impulses, control, and internalized rules maps loosely onto modern ideas, but it is not neuroscience and much of psychoanalytic theory lacks empirical support. Defense mechanisms like rationalization and denial do have modern counterparts (motivated reasoning, self-justification).
- **Internal Family Systems** (Schwartz): treats the mind as "parts" (protectors, wounded parts). Popular in therapy; research base is small. Useful as language for inner conflict ("part of me wants to quit").
- **Transactional Analysis** (Berne): Parent, Adult, Child ego states. A communication shorthand: responding calmly and factually ("Adult") tends to de-escalate. Not a validated model of personality.

## How everyday thinking actually runs (synthesis)
Expectation -> attention to what stands out -> quick intuitive judgment colored by mood and goals -> sometimes deliberate checking -> action -> a story afterward explaining it. Implications: first impressions and framing matter; people are more consistent in stories than in behavior; changing situations often beats changing minds.
