# Exam and Teaching Engine

Use when the user wants quizzes, exams, study plans, or to learn this material.

## Blueprint (100 points; adjust to course)
| Domain | Points |
|---|---|
| Neurophysiology and body states | 15 |
| Attention and memory | 15 |
| Motivation and emotion | 15 |
| Decisions and biases | 15 |
| Habits and persistence | 10 |
| Game and product psychology | 10 |
| Social influence, negotiation, persuasion | 15 |
| Ethics and evidence quality | 5 |
Mix levels: recall, application to new scenarios, analysis of trade-offs, design, evaluation.

## Item-writing rules
1. One concept per question.
2. Clear stem under about 45 words.
3. Wrong options are plausible to a partly informed student, similar in length and grammar.
4. No "all of the above"; avoid negatives.
5. Vary correct-answer position.
6. Application items use new scenarios, not textbook examples.
7. Every item gets a rationale explaining the mechanism and why each wrong option is wrong.
8. Include evidence-quality items (e.g., which finding failed replication).
9. After use, revise items almost everyone or almost no one gets right.

## Formats
Multiple choice; short answer graded against key points; essay with argument, evidence, and counterargument; oral exam with escalating follow-ups; design lab (create an intervention and defend it); critique task (find the mechanism and evidence errors in a piece of advice or copy).

## Design-lab rubric (0-4 each; pass 18/24, no zeros)
Mechanism accuracy; fit to audience state; measurable success and stop criteria; friction handled; ethics built in; counterarguments addressed.

## Sample items (with keys)
1. Caffeine's main alerting mechanism is: A) dopamine release B) adenosine receptor blockade C) serotonin synthesis D) noradrenaline reuptake inhibition. **Key B.** It masks sleep pressure.
2. After a night without sleep, which pattern is best supported? A) stronger prefrontal control B) reduced prefrontal regulation with heightened amygdala reactivity C) uniform decline everywhere D) better encoding. **Key B** (Yoo et al. 2007).
3. A highly involved, knowledgeable audience is most moved by: A) celebrity endorsement B) a 24-hour deadline C) strong comparative evidence that addresses the main counterargument D) upbeat music. **Key C** (central route, ELM).
4. Which finding failed large multi-lab replications? A) testing effect B) ego depletion C) anchoring D) default effects. **Key B.**
5. To raise follow-through on an intention, the best single addition is: A) bigger reward B) a "when-then" plan with time and place C) more reminders D) motivational quote. **Key B** (implementation intentions; moderate-strong).
6. A door-in-the-face request fails most often when the first ask is: A) moderate B) absurdly large, so the retreat isn't seen as a real concession C) from a friend D) in writing. **Key B.**
7. Players of a puzzle game quit at level 12, where completion drops 40%. The best first hypothesis is: A) they've satisfied their need for relatedness B) a difficulty spike frustrating competence C) too many rewards D) loss aversion. **Key B.**
8. Short answer: Why is reducing effort often more effective than increasing rewards? Key points: people discount value steeply by effort; low motivation can still trigger easy behaviors (Fogg); rewards can crowd out intrinsic motivation.

## Study protocol
- Space reviews: 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, ~2 weeks, ~1 month; mix topics within sessions.
- Attempt answers before reading; explain each mechanism in your own words twice.
- Predict your score before each quiz and compare (calibration).
- Every 10 items, invent a new real-world scenario and apply a mechanism without notes.

## Manipulation recognition items (keys included)
9. A caller says they're from your bank, your account is compromised, and you must move money to a "safe account" within the hour. Name two tactics and the safest response. **Key**: authority impersonation and false urgency; hang up and call the number on your card.
10. A partner alternates between intense affection and coldness, and the person feels more attached during the warm periods. Which learning mechanism explains it? **Key**: intermittent (variable) reinforcement and reward prediction error.
11. Short answer: Why do confrontational attacks on a high-control group often backfire for a worried family member? **Key points**: reactance; us-vs-them identity is confirmed; isolation deepens; keeping contact and asking questions works better.
12. Essay: Using four mechanisms, distinguish ethical persuasion from manipulation in a product signup flow, and specify the audit test (templates T10, T11).

## More psychology items (keys included)
13. A habit study participant misses one day. According to Lally et al. (2010), what happens to habit formation? **Key**: little effect; occasional misses don't derail automaticity.
14. Which nudge type is most reliable? A) information-only messages B) defaults C) reminders of social norms D) subliminal cues. **Key B.**
15. A teacher gives gold stars for reading, which students already enjoyed; after stars stop, reading drops. Name the effect and the mechanism. **Key**: overjustification; expected, tangible, controlling rewards shift perceived reason from internal to external (SDT).
16. In a game, a rare drop has a 2% chance. Why does this keep players engaged more than a guaranteed small reward? **Key**: variable ratio reinforcement; reward prediction error makes unexpected rewards produce large dopamine responses.
17. Short answer: Why is "asking why" a weak way to understand someone's behavior? **Key**: limited introspective access; people confabulate (Nisbett & Wilson); ask about specific situations and actions instead.
18. Evidence item: rank by evidence strength: testing effect, power posing hormones, anchoring, ego depletion. **Key**: testing effect and anchoring strong; ego depletion and power posing hormones failed replication.
